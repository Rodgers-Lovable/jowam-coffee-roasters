# Jowam Coffee Roasters

Jowam Coffee Roasters — Website Initial Build

Build a modern, premium website for Jowam Coffee Roasters, a Kenyan specialty coffee business.

This is the first iteration of the website. The goal at this stage is to establish the visual identity, design system, global navigation, reusable components, and the first three fully designed pages:

Home

Menu

Coffee

Additional pages should have routes and simple visually consistent placeholders where appropriate, but DO NOT fully design them yet. They will be specified in later prompts.

1. UNDERSTAND THE BUSINESS FIRST

Jowam Coffee Roasters is NOT simply an online coffee store and NOT simply a specialty coffee roastery.

The business combines:

Café / hospitality

Specialty coffee

Coffee roasting

Retail coffee

Wholesale coffee

Coffee education / training

The core positioning is:

Hospitality-led, with specialty coffee as the anchor.

This distinction should influence the entire website.

The first impression should NOT feel like a technical website made exclusively for coffee enthusiasts.

It should feel like a premium hospitality brand that happens to possess serious specialty-coffee expertise.

The customer should initially think:

This looks like somewhere I want to visit.

Then:

The food and coffee look excellent.

Then:

They actually roast their own specialty coffee.

Then, for interested customers:

There is considerably more depth behind this coffee.

The website therefore needs to balance:

Hospitality + Food + People + Place + Specialty Coffee + Craft

2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The website should feel:

warm

sophisticated

contemporary

distinctly hospitality-led

premium without appearing pretentious

editorial rather than corporate

visually rich

spacious

human

tactile

grounded in coffee and hospitality

appropriate for a high-quality Kenyan brand

Avoid making the site feel like:

a generic SaaS website

a corporate company website

a generic Shopify template

an overly rustic coffee shop

an ultra-minimal fashion brand

a coffee website designed exclusively for coffee experts

The design should feel welcoming to an ordinary café customer while still possessing enough sophistication to satisfy someone knowledgeable about specialty coffee.

5. PHOTOGRAPHY STYLE

Avoid generic stock-photo aesthetics.

Images should feel:

candid

editorial

warm

natural

observational

premium

textural

authentic

Prefer moments where people are naturally interacting rather than looking at the camera.

Examples:

Instead of an isolated croissant:

Show coffee + pastry + table + hand + morning environment.

Instead of a portrait of a barista staring at camera:

Show the barista preparing espresso.

Instead of an empty café:

Show the café being used.

Photography should tell stories.

6. LAYOUT PHILOSOPHY

Avoid excessive use of:

cards

rounded containers

icon grids

gradients

glassmorphism

decorative UI

floating widgets

generic "features" sections

The site should rely primarily on:

photography + typography + whitespace + composition

Use editorial layouts.

Examples:

large asymmetric image/text sections

overlapping compositions where appropriate

tall portrait photography

wide cinematic photography

restrained product grids

large typographic statements

Not every section needs a container.

Allow the page to breathe.

7. VISUAL RHYTHM

Avoid repeating the same section structure throughout the website.

Do NOT create:

image-left/text-right
image-left/text-right
image-left/text-right

for every section.

Alternate between:

full-width imagery

asymmetric layouts

text-only statements

product grids

horizontal photography

dark roastery sections

warm hospitality sections

whitespace-heavy editorial sections

The page should have visual rhythm.

8. TYPOGRAPHY

Use an elegant editorial typography system.

Use:

expressive display typography for large headlines

highly readable typography for body copy

restrained uppercase labels where useful

generous line spacing

strong hierarchy

Large headlines can occupy substantial space.

Avoid excessively small typography.

Avoid the visual language of startup/SaaS websites.

Typography should contribute significantly to the premium hospitality feeling.

9. COLOUR SYSTEM

Create a warm, coffee-inspired but sophisticated palette.

Do NOT simply make everything brown.

Use a restrained system inspired by:

warm cream

natural paper

deep espresso

charcoal

muted earthy tones

subtle coffee/cherry accents

Allow photography to provide much of the colour.

Use darker backgrounds selectively for:

roasting

wholesale

important transitions

The café/hospitality portions should generally feel warmer and lighter.

The roastery can feel darker, tactile and more industrial.

10. MOTION

Motion should be restrained and premium.

Use:

subtle image reveals

gentle fade/slide transitions

smooth hover interactions

restrained text reveals

subtle image scaling

smooth page transitions where appropriate

Avoid:

excessive parallax

bouncing elements

gimmicky animations

animated coffee beans

excessive scroll effects

Motion should reinforce quality.

25. FINAL QUALITY CHECK

Before considering this first iteration complete, evaluate the website against these questions:

Home

Does it make me want to visit Jowam?

Does the food look desirable?

Do I quickly understand that Jowam roasts specialty coffee?

Does the site feel human?

Menu

Can I quickly find what I want?

Are prices and descriptions readable?

Does food photography create appetite without overwhelming usability?

Does the coffee section feel special?

Coffee

Can a beginner understand it?

Can an enthusiast find depth?

Do I understand that Jowam roasts its own coffee?

Do I understand that origin and processing matter?

Can I naturally move toward purchasing coffee?

Overall

Does this feel like a hospitality brand rather than an online coffee catalogue?

Does specialty coffee remain a strong anchor?

Is photography carrying meaningful storytelling?

Does the design avoid generic cards and startup UI?

Does it feel premium without feeling inaccessible?

Does every major section have a clear reason to exist?

If not, revise before finishing.
